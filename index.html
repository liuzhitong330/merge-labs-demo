Can yesterday’s calibration cut today’s bench burden?

What published functional-ultrasound BMI results suggest about saving experimental time without giving up endpoint performance.

Cathy LiuResearch Associate candidateIndependent exploratory analysis

Calibration is an operational bottleneck in brain–machine interface experiments. I asked a narrow question with direct consequences for bench execution: if a decoder carries structure forward from a previous session, where does that reclaimed trial budget become scientifically useful?

Methods & limitations

What was analyzed

I transcribed the published group means for trials-to-significance and endpoint performance from Griggs, Norman et al. (2024), then calculated absolute and percentage trial reductions for five task × subject cohorts. This is a transparent secondary analysis of reported summary statistics, not a claim to have reprocessed the 34.3 GB imaging archive.

What the result does not prove

Two animals and a small number of sessions cannot establish clinical generalizability. Group means hide session-level variability, and trials are only a partial proxy for experimental cost. The useful result is a testable operational hypothesis: preserve alignment and validate transfer before collecting a full cold-start calibration set.
